## Flagged readings/sources worth considering (not yet slotted in)

- **Whitney Battle-Baptiste & Britt Rusert, eds., *W.E.B. Du Bois's Data Portraits*** — Du Bois's 1900 Paris Exposition infographics on Black life. Strong fit for either the data-viz week (10) or the mapping week (11); arguably a *better* anchor for those weeks than Yau alone, since it's slavery/Black-history-specific.
- **Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database (slavevoyages.org)** — a natural recurring dataset for the labs, especially Weeks 4, 9, and 11 (mapping forced migration).
- A **second reaction piece to Time on the Cross** beyond Gutman — e.g. something from the *Journal of Economic History* roundtable (which Blevins' Week 3 already gestures at via the Baptist roundtable) — could round out Week 4 with a more recent economic-historian's retrospective on the Fogel/Engerman debate.

## One structural note
Several Thursdays (Weeks 3, 4, 5, 8, 9) assume you want hands-on Python; if you decide against leaning into it, those labs could become guided walkthroughs of existing public projects (Mapping the Gay Guides, slavevoyages.org, Du Bois's portraits) instead of build-it-yourself sessions — same conceptual payload, no coding requirement.
